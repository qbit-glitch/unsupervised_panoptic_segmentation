# Summary

The paper proposes a monocular-only pseudo-label pipeline for scene-centric unsupervised panoptic segmentation, replacing CUPS's stereo+optical-flow construction with frozen single-frame priors composed via cross-modal agreement. The two technical contributions are (i) DCFA, a ~225K-parameter residual MLP adapter that conditions a frozen CAUSE-TR semantic code on monocular depth via a sinusoidal-encoded depth shift, and (ii) SIMCF, a learning-free three-step filter (per-region majority class, DINOv3-similarity merge, depth-implausibility void). On Cityscapes val under the 27-class CAUSE+Hungarian protocol the system reports 35.83 PQ (+8.03 over CUPS published), with a contribution-isolated +3.07 PQ over a matched monocular baseline (32.76 PQ), and large zero-shot gains on KITTI/Waymo/Mapillary.

# Strengths

- **Honest and well-controlled contribution-isolation framing.** The authors construct a matched monocular baseline (32.76 PQ, same Stage-2/3 trainer, same DINOv3 ViT-B/16 backbone, same K=80 raw labels) and explicitly state the +3.07 PQ delta is the only quantity attributable to DCFA+SIMCF; the +5.0 PQ from the baseline-vs-CUPS gap is honestly attributed to the pseudo-label-source substitution + backbone change. This is far more rigorous than the typical unsupervised panoptic submission.
- **The conditioning-mechanism ablation (Table 5) is unusually principled.** Comparing DCFA against (i) DepthG drop-in (-11.21 PQ), (ii) loss-side P(z) without preserve (-11.92), (iii) loss-side+preserve (23.83) at matched parameter count actually isolates the architectural axis (residual feature-level injection) from the loss axis (depth-correlation) and from the preservation axis. The 1.39 PQ residual gap between DCFA and loss-side+preserve, after both axes are matched, is the cleanest possible operationalization of "architectural depth conditioning matters".
- **SIMCF sensitivity sweep (Appendix C) is the right kind of robustness check.** A 0.71 PQ band across +/-12% threshold perturbation, against a +1.31 PQ Stage-1 contribution, is a real argument that SIMCF is not tuned-on-test.
- **Cross-dataset transfer is strong and reported under multiple protocols.** Reporting both 19-class and 27-class numbers on Mapillary/Waymo, and explicitly explaining why 27-class drops (extra Cityscapes-only categories being averaged in), shows protocol awareness.
- **The phi mapping is precisely specified as no-ground-truth (Appendix B.6).** Carefully calling out that "27" means CAUSE concept prototypes (not Cityscapes classes) and that the only ground-truth-derived component is the Hungarian map at metric time is the right level of pedantry for this subarea, where reviewers regularly catch label-leakage bugs.
- **Appropriate scoping of the contribution.** The introduction does not oversell; the paper repeatedly resists the temptation to claim the +8.03 PQ as "the method's contribution."
- **Hardware accessibility.** ~58 GPU-hours on 2x consumer 1080 Ti is a reproducibility win against CUPS's stereo-rig requirement, and is consistent with the monocular-democratization framing.
- **Failure-mode taxonomy (Table B.4) is non-defensive.** The four-mode breakdown (over-fragmentation, under-detection, class confusion, boundary noise) with concrete proposed mitigations is the kind of self-critical analysis that distinguishes a mature submission.
- **Per-class table (Table E.1) does not hide the dead classes.** Six vocabulary-dead classes (parking, guard rail, tunnel, polegroup, caravan, trailer) at 0.0 PQ are explicitly listed and discussed.

# Weaknesses

(severity-ranked, highest first)

- **(High) The headline +8.03 PQ vs. CUPS confounds three orthogonal factors and the paper acknowledges but does not resolve this.** The author's clarification (CVPR-track advisor approved skipping the same-backbone CUPS rerun) explains why limitation (iv) exists, but for a NeurIPS submission this is still a gap. The +8.03 PQ in Table 1 spans (a) DINOv3 ViT-B/16 vs. CUPS's DINO ResNet-50, (b) monocular vs. stereo+flow pseudo-label substrate, and (c) DCFA+SIMCF. Only (c) is isolated (+3.07). The remaining +5.0 PQ is described as "pseudo-label-source substitution alone" but it is in fact source + backbone capacity. Title and abstract should not lead with the 35.83 number (or should lead with it under a clearly-labeled multi-confound caveat) given that the same-backbone CUPS rerun is missing.
- **(High) DCFA's novelty claim is narrow but probably defensible — though the paper undersells the burden of proof.** The claim "first parameter-efficient adapter that conditions a frozen unsupervised semantic code on monocular depth via residual feature-level injection" is heavily qualified ("first . . . unsupervised . . . residual . . . feature-level"). On its face, depth-conditioned residual MLPs over frozen ViT features are a well-trodden pattern (FiLM, LoRA-style adapters, RGB-D fusion adapters such as the one in DFormerv2). DepthG already conditions on depth via a correlation loss. The novelty rests entirely on (i) the substrate being a frozen unsupervised concept-clusterbook code (90-D CAUSE-TR), and (ii) the conditioning being feature-level rather than loss-level. Table 5 does isolate the latter cleanly (+1.39 PQ over loss-side+preserve), which is a real argument. But the paper should explicitly compare DCFA against at least one parameter-efficient adapter baseline (e.g., a FiLM adapter, an LoRA-style query/value injector, or a depth-as-extra-token concatenation) at matched parameter budget; absent that, "first parameter-efficient adapter" is a claim about the unoccupied corner of a larger design space, not a technical contribution per se.
- **(High) SIMCF is principled in spirit but ad-hoc in instantiation.** The three checks (majority class within instance, DINOv3-cosine merge, +/-eta-sigma depth void) each correspond to an interpretable failure mode (mixed-class targets, depth over-fragmentation, depth-implausible assignments). But the contribution-isolated effect of SIMCF alone in Table 4 is +0.73 PQ (24.54 -> 25.27), and Both is only +0.58 PQ over either single component. The component-complementarity argument is statistically weak at this scale (no error bars; single seed; +0.05 PQ separation between the two singletons). A cleaner ablation would isolate steps A/B/C of SIMCF independently and report which one contributes — currently the reader cannot tell whether the merge step (depth over-fragmentation repair, the most novel) or the depth-implausibility void (the most ad-hoc) does the work.
- **(High) Single-seed Stage-3 result.** Limitation (v) admits this; for a +3.07 PQ contribution-isolation claim against a noisy training pipeline (Cascade Mask R-CNN + DropLoss + EMA self-training + copy-paste), single-seed numbers cannot distinguish a +3.07 effect from seed-variance. Multi-seed runs are not optional at NeurIPS for an isolation claim of this size, especially when each seed costs only ~30 GPU-hours (12+16) on consumer hardware.
- **(Medium) DepthPro is potentially load-bearing, and the depth-model ablation is ambiguous.** Table B.3 shows DA v3 at tau=0.03 (27.37 PQ) actually beats DepthPro at the chosen tau=0.20 (26.13 PQ) on raw K=80, and DepthPro at tau=0.01 reaches 27.81. The choice of (DepthPro, tau=0.20) is justified by "training-data quality" (22 vs 57 instances/image) rather than pseudo-label PQ. This is a defensible argument but is not validated by a Stage-2/3 retraining ablation: there is no row showing what happens when the trainer is fed the tau=0.01 fragmented labels. Without that, the depth-source choice is asserted, not demonstrated.
- **(Medium) Honest weakness: monocular-baseline-vs-CUPS provenance is murky.** The 32.76 PQ monocular baseline shares Stage-2/3 trainer and DINOv3 ViT-B/16 backbone with the full method. But it is not clear from the text whether this baseline is the result of running the CUPS Stage-2/3 trainer on raw K=80 DepthPro/CAUSE labels, OR whether something more nuanced was done. If the former, the paper has effectively constructed a strong "monocular CUPS baseline" that itself is a contribution worth foregrounding more (since 32.76 already beats published CUPS by +5 PQ); if the latter, the baseline construction needs more documentation.
- **(Medium) Cross-dataset transfer numbers are dramatic and warrant scrutiny.** +9.4 PQ on KITTI, +17.1 PQ on Waymo over CUPS is a very large gap. Some of this is real (the Stage-3 network with DINOv3 ViT-B/16 is a stronger inductive prior than CUPS's ResNet-50), but 17 PQ on Waymo is large enough that I would want to know: (a) is the Waymo evaluation set the same as CUPS's? (b) is the 27-class Hungarian assignment the same protocol? (c) what fraction of the +17 PQ is attributable to the stronger backbone? The footnote about Waymo via CUPS loader is helpful but a brief same-backbone CUPS rerun on KITTI alone (which is small, 200 images) would dispel the "backbone confound" interpretation cheaply.
- **(Medium) The +1.31 PQ Stage-1 -> +3.07 PQ Stage-3 amplification is asserted, not explained.** The paper notes the DCFA+SIMCF advantage "compounds through Stage-2/3 training" (Table 3). This is an interesting empirical observation — Stage-2/3 amplifies pseudo-label quality differences super-linearly — but the paper does not explain why or test the inverse claim. A Stage-3 training run on raw K=80 pseudo-labels (limitation (vi)) would test whether DCFA+SIMCF actually affects asymptotic trained PQ or merely accelerates convergence. This is the single experiment that would most strengthen the paper.
- **(Low) DCFA Loss = DepthG correlation + L2 preservation. The "preservation term" novelty.** The paper emphasizes preservation as a key design choice (Table 5 shows DepthG without preservation collapses to 13.33 PQ). This is genuinely useful empirical knowledge, but conceptually a preservation/identity-anchor term is standard in adapter literature (LoRA, prefix-tuning, IA3). The paper should cite parameter-efficient transfer learning more broadly to position the preservation term.
- **(Low) Notational/presentation issues.** (i) Limitation (i) "six dead classes" and the per-class table list seven vocabulary-coverage cases (parking, guard rail, tunnel, polegroup, caravan, trailer + motorcycle which is 0.1 PQ); the discussion in §6 should reconcile this. (ii) Table 5 footnote "Both axes are required for the +0.68 PQ over raw" is confusing — DCFA gives 25.22 - 24.54 = +0.68 PQ on Stage-1 pseudo-labels, but the Stage-3 number (35.83 - 32.76 = +3.07) is what the abstract emphasizes; the +0.68 is for DCFA-only at Stage-1, not DCFA+SIMCF at Stage-3.
- **(Low) DepthG drop-in row in Table 5 may be unfair.** "DepthG (drop-in)" loads the official DepthG ViT-B/8 checkpoint as a Stage-1 generator under the paper's pipeline. ViT-B/8 vs. DINOv2 ViT-B/14 is a substrate change; the 13.33 PQ may reflect substrate mismatch rather than DepthG-the-method failing in this pipeline. A clean DepthG control would re-train DepthG on DINOv2 ViT-B/14 features. This is worth a footnote.

# Soundness (1-4)

**3 — Good but not flawless.** The contribution-isolation construction (matched Stage-2/3, matched backbone, varying only Stage-1 source) is the right experimental design and is faithfully reported. The conditioning-mechanism decomposition (Table 5) is unusually clean. However, single-seed Stage-3, the missing same-backbone CUPS control, the absent step-by-step SIMCF ablation, and the depth-source choice driven by instance counts rather than trainer outcome each weaken specific claims. None of these is fatal individually; collectively they cap soundness below 4.

# Presentation (1-4)

**3 — Clear, well-structured, occasionally cluttered.** Section structure follows NeurIPS norms. Tables are well-captioned and consistently flag train/val protocol. The Appendix is comprehensive (implementation, sensitivity, depth ablation, OOD, per-class, broader impact). Minor issues: limitation (iv) is buried in a single dense paragraph; the headline 35.83 vs. 27.80 framing in the abstract risks oversell that the body then carefully walks back; some tables (especially Table 1 and Table 5) would benefit from explicit "what this row holds fixed" annotations rather than running prose. The DCFA architecture would be clarified by a small side panel showing input/output shapes.

# Contribution (1-4)

**3 — Solid contribution to a small but important subarea.** This is the first credible monocular-only scene-centric unsupervised panoptic system that beats stereo-based CUPS under controlled comparison. DCFA is a focused architectural contribution (residual depth-conditioned adapter on a frozen unsupervised concept code), and SIMCF is a useful learning-free recipe. Neither is a methodological breakthrough on the scale of CUPS or U2Seg, but together they do enable a new operating regime (monocular-only pseudo-label generation) that the field needs. The +3.07 PQ contribution-isolated improvement is real but modest; the headline +8.03 PQ confounds backbone and source. I would call this a "useful, careful, slightly over-claimed contribution" rather than a paradigm-defining one.

# Overall (1-10)

**6 — Borderline (lean accept).** This is a careful, well-controlled, honestly-scoped paper that makes a real contribution to monocular unsupervised panoptic segmentation and establishes a new operating point on Cityscapes. The contribution-isolation framing is the best I have seen in this subarea in the past year, and the conditioning-mechanism decomposition (Table 5) is genuinely informative. Against this, the headline 35.83 PQ vs. 27.80 PQ framing is misleading without the same-backbone CUPS rerun (which the author's advisor explicitly waived but which I, as a NeurIPS reviewer, would still want); the +3.07 PQ contribution-isolated claim rests on a single seed; SIMCF is not ablated step-by-step; and DCFA's novelty boils down to a narrow but defensible "first feature-level residual adapter on a frozen unsupervised clusterbook code with depth conditioning" claim that the paper undersells the burden of proof for. I would push for acceptance if the authors (a) add multi-seed Stage-3 runs (3 seeds at minimum), (b) add a Stage-3 control trained on raw K=80 to confirm the +1.31 -> +3.07 amplification claim, (c) add at least one parameter-efficient-adapter baseline (FiLM or LoRA-style depth injector) to substantiate the DCFA novelty claim, and (d) decompose SIMCF's three steps. Without (a) and (b) at minimum, the paper is borderline — the controlled comparison framing is genuinely strong, but the asymptotic behavior of the trainer on raw vs. filtered pseudo-labels is exactly the question the paper raises and does not answer. Lean accept: the framing and execution are high-quality enough that the field will benefit from reading this work even with the open questions, and the missing experiments are straightforward to add for the camera-ready or revision.

# Confidence (1-5)

**4 — Very confident.** I have reviewed CUPS, CutLER, U2Seg, DepthG, and CAUSE in this venue and predecessor venues; I am familiar with the CUPS evaluation protocol (27-class CAUSE + Hungarian) and the typical failure modes of depth-based instance separation. I am less certain about the precise novelty boundary between DCFA and recent feature-level depth-conditioning adapters in the supervised RGB-D fusion literature (DFormerv2 territory), which is why I am at 4 not 5.

# Questions for authors

1. **Multi-seed.** The paper reports a single Stage-3 run for both the matched monocular baseline (32.76) and the full method (35.83). Given the noise of CUPS-style trainers (Cascade Mask R-CNN + DropLoss + 3 rounds of EMA self-training), can you report mean/std over at least 3 seeds for both the baseline and the full method? Without this, +3.07 PQ contribution-isolation is one seed difference. (You acknowledge this in limitation (v); please act on it.)

2. **SIMCF step-wise ablation.** Table 4 toggles SIMCF as a single switch. SIMCF has three sequential steps (A: per-instance majority class, B: DINOv3-cosine merge, C: depth-implausibility void). Can you report a step-wise ablation isolating which of A/B/C carries the +1.0 PQ Stage-1 contribution, and which (if any) is dispensable? This would substantively strengthen the principled-vs-ad-hoc question for SIMCF.

3. **DCFA vs. parameter-efficient-adapter baselines.** The novelty claim is "first feature-level residual adapter on a frozen unsupervised concept-clusterbook code with monocular depth conditioning." Have you compared DCFA against (a) a FiLM-style depth-conditioned modulation layer on the same frozen code, or (b) a LoRA-style low-rank depth injector? At matched parameter budget (~225K), what is the marginal contribution of the specific DCFA architecture (concat-MLP-residual) vs. these alternative parameter-efficient designs? Without one such baseline, "first" reads as "novel by virtue of the unoccupied corner."

4. **Stage-3 amplification mechanism.** Table 3 shows the +1.31 PQ Stage-1 contribution amplifying to +3.07 PQ at Stage-3. Can you train Stage-3 on the raw K=80 pseudo-labels (no DCFA, no SIMCF) and report PQ? Limitation (vi) flags this. Does DCFA+SIMCF affect asymptotic trained PQ, or only convergence speed and rare-class recall? This is the single experiment that would most clarify the paper's contribution.

5. **Same-backbone CUPS rerun (or its closest substitute).** I understand from the meta-information that your CVPR-track advisor approved skipping the full same-backbone CUPS rerun, but: even a partial control — e.g., running CUPS's released stereo+flow pseudo-labels through your Stage-2/3 trainer with the DINOv3 ViT-B/16 backbone — would isolate the backbone effect and let the reader partition the +5 PQ baseline-vs-CUPS gap into source vs. backbone. Is this control feasible for the rebuttal?

6. **Cross-dataset Waymo +17.1 PQ.** This is a very large transfer gap. Are the Waymo evaluation set, Hungarian protocol, and 27-class taxonomy identical to CUPS's? What fraction of the +17.1 PQ would you attribute to (i) DCFA+SIMCF, (ii) the DINOv3 backbone, (iii) the differing pseudo-label source, vs. (iv) any difference in the Waymo loader/evaluation pipeline? A brief decomposition would let the reader trust the headline transfer numbers.
