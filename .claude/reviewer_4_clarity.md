# NeurIPS 2026 Review — Reviewer 4 (Clarity, Presentation, Accessibility)

## Summary

The paper proposes a monocular pseudo-label pipeline for unsupervised panoptic segmentation on driving scenes. It removes stereo and optical-flow dependencies that the prior CUPS pipeline required at pseudo-label time, and replaces them with two compact mechanisms: DCFA, a ~225K-parameter residual adapter that conditions a frozen unsupervised semantic code on monocular depth; and SIMCF, a learning-free three-step filter (semantic uniformity, feature-similarity merge, depth-implausibility rejection) that cleans candidate panoptic labels. With these labels feeding the standard CUPS Stage-2/3 trainer (Cascade Mask R-CNN over a frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16 backbone, DropLoss, copy-paste, 3 rounds of EMA self-training), the system reaches 35.83 PQ on Cityscapes val. The same Stage-2/3 trainer reaches 32.76 PQ on raw monocular pseudo-labels with neither DCFA nor SIMCF, making the contribution-isolated effect of the two mechanisms +3.07 PQ. Cross-dataset results (KITTI 34.85, Waymo 43.51, Mapillary 39.19 PQ) and OOD diagnostics (MOTS, COCO-Stuff-27) are reported, plus an honest 4-mode failure analysis and per-class breakdown in the appendix.

I read both the main paper (~7 pages) and the supplementary material (~15 pages).

## Strengths

**Abstract follows a clean Gap → Insight → Punch arc.** The opener names the problem (monocular footage excludes long tail) in one sentence, the insight (cross-modal agreement among single-frame priors recovers stereo/flow redundancy) in the second, the mechanisms (DCFA + SIMCF, with parameter counts) in the third, and the headline numbers at the stress positions: "32.76 PQ … 35.83 PQ … +3.07 PQ contribution-isolated … +5.0 PQ pseudo-label-source substitution." The two-axis decomposition of the headline gap is honest and easy to read. Better than 90% of NeurIPS abstracts I see.

**Contribution-isolation framing is the paper's standout move.** The decision to report both an external comparison (vs published CUPS 27.80) AND a controlled in-paper baseline (matched Stage-2/3 trainer, same backbone, only the Stage-1 source differs) is exactly the right way to defuse the obvious reviewer attack ("your gain is just the bigger backbone"). The +3.07 PQ delta is then attributable to DCFA+SIMCF, and the +5.0 PQ remainder is attributed to pseudo-label substitution — cleanly partitioned. This shows up consistently in the abstract, intro contribution list, Table 1, the pipeline progression table, and the limitations section. The repetition is welcome.

**SIMCF's three steps (A/B/C) are motivated before defined.** The opening sentence of §3.4 names three concrete failure modes ("a depth fragment may cut through a single object, a proposal may mix pseudo-classes, or a semantic assignment may sit at a depth far from its pseudo-class range"), and each then maps 1:1 to step A, B, or C. Reader knows *why* each step exists before seeing *what* it does.

**DCFA is explained with intuition before formalism.** §3.3 opens with the goal ("nudging cluster boundaries toward depth-consistent regions where appearance fails"), then walks through (i) why scalar depth needs to be lifted, (ii) the sinusoidal encoding with eight frequencies, (iii) the residual update, (iv) zero-init makes step 0 identity. The training-loss block (Eq. 4) lands after the architecture has been internalised. The residual+preserve framing is intuitive.

**Failure mode appendix (§A.5) disarms reviewer attacks rather than inviting them.** The 4-mode taxonomy (over-fragmentation, under-detection, class confusion, boundary noise) plus the mechanism+mitigation table is a textbook example of how to do this well. Each mode has a *named mechanism* (Cascade Mask R-CNN confidence threshold, ego-vehicle pseudo-class, stride-14 ViT upsampling argmax) and a *concrete mitigation*. A reviewer who would otherwise spend a paragraph attacking "the pedestrian crowd failure" instead reads that the authors already know about it, named it as a mechanism (depth co-planarity in the depth branch), and proposed the fix (top-K-per-image rule). The deterministic-argmax selection script being committed in the repo (option_3 build script cited in caption) reinforces honesty.

**Limitations (§6) are specific and defensible.** Six numbered points, three "method-level" and three "experimental controls left for future work." Each is concrete: dead classes, COCO-Stuff vocab boundary, pedestrian crowd in depth branch, missing same-backbone CUPS rerun, single-seed Stage-3 number, missing Stage-3-from-raw control. No vague hand-waving. The author honestly states "the +3.07 PQ delta is the part attributable to DCFA+SIMCF" — which is exactly the right framing given the missing same-backbone CUPS run.

**Pipeline progression table (Table 2) tells a clean story.** Four rows, each adding one component, "+1.31 PQ Stage-1 advantage grows to +3.07 PQ at the trained-network level." The compounding-through-training narrative is the paper's most valuable rhetorical move because it converts a small Stage-1 effect (+1.31) into a substantial trained-model effect (+3.07).

**Cross-dataset generalization is a strong asset.** The KITTI/Waymo/Mapillary numbers (+9.4 / +17.1 / not-reported by CUPS) make the paper feel like a method, not a dataset-specific tweak. The two-row 19-class/27-class reporting on Mapillary and Waymo is the right call (one for CUPS-comparable, one for in-domain disclosure).

**Visualization conventions appendix (§A.7) is exemplary.** Three rendering choices spelled out (Hungarian-aligned palette, void inpainting on overlay only, shuffled tab20 with 45% blend), each with a one-sentence rationale, and the explicit assurance that "None of the three choices touches the panoptic prediction itself; PQ numbers in the main paper are unaffected." This is the right way to handle the "are the pretty pictures honest" question.

**Honest ablation gap (§A.4 missing +DCFA-only column).** The paper explicitly admits "the existing checkpoint set does not contain a Stage-3 model trained on DCFA-corrected pseudo-labels with SIMCF disabled, and we did not regenerate one for this submission." Cleaner than burying it.

**Per-class table (§A.6) handles the dead-class issue gracefully.** Six dead classes get 0/0/0 rows but are explicitly named in the caption as "vocabulary-dead," and the discussion paragraph names them and explains they "lie outside the standard Cityscapes-19 panoptic benchmark." The "twenty active classes" reframe quietly removes them from the success-claim denominator without hiding them.

## Weaknesses

**The abstract's parameter count is unverifiable from the paper.** Abstract says "~225K parameters." §3.3 (Eq. 4 surroundings) and Implementation Details §B.2 also say "~225K." Good — these are mutually consistent. **However**, the paper does not derive 225K from the architecture spec. With the stated MLP `r: R^106 → R^384 → R^384 → R^90`, two LayerNorms, ReLU activations, and zero-initialised output projection, a careful reader has to reconstruct the count themselves (≈ 41K + 148K + 35K ≈ 223K). Add a one-paragraph derivation in §B.2 so a reviewer can verify in 10 seconds.

**Intro contribution list is borderline generic until contribution 3 lands.** Bullet 1 ("Monocular pseudo-label pipeline that removes calibrated stereo and optical flow") and bullet 2 ("Two compact mechanisms") could appear in many papers. Bullet 3 ("contribution-isolated +3.07 PQ over a matched monocular baseline") is the specific, verifiable one and is what makes the paper feel real. Consider rewriting bullet 1 to lead with the specific principle ("composed through cross-modal agreement among frozen single-frame priors") rather than the negative ("removes calibrated stereo"). Right now the negative framing in bullets 1–2 makes them sound like ablations rather than contributions.

**Pipeline overview figure (Fig. 1) is referenced once and then never again from the main paper.** §3.1 ¶2 says "Figure 1 summarizes the two-stage pipeline" and after that the figure does no further work. For a reader who doesn't go back to the figure, the main-text pipeline description (¶3 of §3.1) repeats the same content. Either thread the figure into §3.2/§3.3 ("the depth-conditioned residual r in the upper-right of Figure 1") or compress §3.1 ¶3 since the figure already covers it. Right now the figure feels like decoration.

**Table 4 (Conditioning ablation) caption is too dense.** "All rows share the same downstream pipeline; only how depth enters the model varies" is the *only* sentence in the caption that helps a reader who jumps to the table. The four conditioning conditions (DepthG official, Loss-side P(z) no preserve, Loss-side P(z)+preserve, DCFA) need a compact glossary in the caption itself, because the main-text walk-through is currently the only place where "Loss-side P(z)" is defined. Consider a 2-line caption: "DepthG = official end-to-end checkpoint; Loss-side P(z) = learnable 90→90 trained with depth-correlation loss only; +preserve = adds λ_p = 20 preservation; DCFA = our depth-conditioned residual."

**Table 1 is dense and hard to scan.** Eight columns (Method, Training data, PL inputs, Eval cls., PQ, SQ, RQ, PQ_Th, PQ_St), small font, resized to text width. The most important column (PQ) is visually buried in the middle. Consider: (i) bolding the PQ column header so the eye finds it; (ii) using `\bfseries` on the PQ values for "Ours" rather than just on the entire row; (iii) adding a thin horizontal rule between "Monocular baseline" and "Ours" to visually echo the contribution-isolated comparison the prose makes. Right now the +3.07 PQ contribution-isolated jump (32.76 → 35.83) is in the table but a reader has to do mental arithmetic.

**Cross-dataset table (Table 5) has an awkward "vs CUPS" delta row.** The "vs CUPS" deltas (+9.4, +17.1) at the bottom are in green text and italic, which is unusual styling that may confuse colour-blind readers and looks promotional. Consider replacing the green with a simple "Δ vs. CUPS" row in normal styling.

**SIMCF threshold sensitivity (§4.2 paragraph + Appendix C) is good but could be one notch sharper.** The paragraph correctly notes "0.71-point PQ band substantially smaller than the +1.31 PQ gap." Two improvements: (i) state what the band's centre is (25.87 baseline) so the band is anchored; (ii) note explicitly that *tighter* thresholds slightly improve PQ (25.98 vs 25.87 baseline), which means the paper setting is not even at the sweep optimum — a strong robustness signal that the current text undersells.

**The Conclusion's opening sentence does too much work.** "Frozen monocular priors composed by cross-modal agreement suffice to seed the CUPS training recipe" is a strong philosophical opener but reads slightly opaque on first parse. A reader who hasn't internalised "agreement-as-operation" by §7 may bounce off it. Consider following with a one-sentence paraphrase: "Each prior fails on its own; the pipeline uses these failure modes against each other so that surviving labels satisfy multiple priors simultaneously."

**Stage-1 trace figure (Fig. A.1) row 2 caption is good but cryptic for a reader who skipped to the appendix.** "Row 2 (hamburg) is the canonical co-planar pedestrian failure case discussed in the main paper's Conclusion section" — a non-Cityscapes-fluent reviewer may not know "hamburg" is a Cityscapes city subset, and "canonical" suggests the reader has been told before. Replace with: "Row 2 (image hamburg/000000_xx) shows the co-planar pedestrian crowd failure mode (§6 Limitations, mode 3): three pedestrians at approximately the same depth are merged into a single instance because the depth gradient between them is below threshold."

**Notation drift on G/F.** Eq. 1 introduces $G$ (pseudo-label generator) and $F$ (test-time network). §3.5 and §6 then casually use "Stage-1 / Stage-2 / Stage-3" without re-grounding to G/F. A reader skimming §6 may forget that "Stage-3 EMA self-training" is the round that produces $F$. The phrase "use the final-round student as the network $F$ in Eq. 1" appears ONCE in §3.5 — extend it to a brief reminder at the start of §6 ("our headline 35.83 PQ is the final $F$").

**Figure 2 (DCFA adapter training) is referenced before it appears.** §3.3 ¶1 cites "Figure 2(b)" but the LaTeX places this inside §3.2's `\begin{figure}` environment and §3.2 says "Figure 2(a) shows…" — a reader who reads sequentially encounters the figure only when it floats and has to scroll back. This is technically correct LaTeX but feels chunky. Compress §3.2 and §3.3's prose around the figure or split the subfigure into two separate figures (one in §3.2, one in §3.3).

**§A.7 Visualization Conventions buried at the END of the appendix.** This subsection should be referenced earlier — ideally as a footnote on the first qualitative figure that uses the conventions (Fig. A.2 Stage-3 predictions). Right now a reviewer who reads §A.2 without scrolling to §A.7 may wonder why the colours are "not standard Cityscapes." Add a forward reference in the Fig. A.2 caption: "Visualization conventions described in §A.7."

**§A.4 caption ends with a clause that requires extra parsing.** "Differences should be read as the joint effect of (i) DCFA-corrected pseudo-labels, (ii) SIMCF instance/depth filtering, and (iii) the DINOv3 ViT-B/16 backbone replacing CUPS' DINO ResNet-50." This is technically the right disclaimer but it tells the reader the figure is *less* informative than it looks (because it bundles three changes). Better: explicitly state what *cannot* be concluded from this figure ("this side-by-side cannot isolate DCFA's contribution from the backbone change, which is why Table 4 reports controlled ablations").

**Failure-mode mitigation column reads like internal TODO list.** "Enforce A_min at the merger (drop instances <200 px before assigning IDs)" / "Replace the absolute confidence threshold with a top-K-per-image rule" / etc. These are fine but read as engineering wishes rather than a future-research roadmap. Consider rephrasing as research questions ("Whether area filtering at the merger stage transfers the Stage-1 robustness to deployment-time outputs is open") to elevate the register.

## Soundness (1–4)
**3** — The main empirical claims rest on careful controlled comparisons (matched Stage-2/3 trainer, same backbone) and the contribution-isolation framing is rigorously executed. The paper is honest about the missing controls (same-backbone CUPS rerun, multi-seed Stage-3, raw-K=80 Stage-3). Soundness is not a 4 because (i) the headline 35.83 PQ is single-seed, (ii) one ablation comparison (the +DCFA-only Stage-3 column in §A.4) is absent, and (iii) the parameter count (~225K) is asserted but not derived inline.

## Presentation (1–4)
**3** — Above-average for NeurIPS. The narrative arc (Gap → Insight → Punch in the abstract; Stage-1 progression in Table 2; failure modes with named mechanisms) is well-executed. The contribution-isolation framing is repeated consistently across sections. Tables would benefit from minor reformatting (bolder PQ column, tighter caption glossaries) and figures from forward references (§A.7) and consolidated placement around §3.2/§3.3. Not a 4 because of the figure-cross-reference flow issues, the dense Table 1 layout, and the unusual green-italic styling of delta rows.

## Contribution (1–4)
**3** — DCFA is positioned as the *first* feature-level residual adapter applied to a frozen unsupervised concept-clusterbook code for monocular depth conditioning. The novelty axis is plausible (qualified against DepthG's loss-side end-to-end finetuning) and the +3.07 PQ contribution-isolated improvement is a real number on a real benchmark. SIMCF is honestly a learning-free filter — clearly engineering, but its ablation contribution is also honestly small (+0.05 PQ over DCFA-only at Stage-1). The headline 35.83 PQ surpasses S2-UniSeg's 25.4 (concurrent monocular competitor) and CUPS's 27.80 (stereo+flow); even the controlled monocular baseline (32.76) beats the prior published number. Strong contribution. Not a 4 because (i) the same-backbone CUPS rerun isn't done (per author's stated scope), so part of the headline gap is attributed to confounded factors that the +3.07 PQ delta doesn't cover; (ii) the SIMCF mechanism is straightforward.

## Overall (1–10)
**7 (Weak Accept)** — The paper makes a clean, well-isolated empirical contribution on a problem (monocular unsupervised panoptic) of growing relevance. The writing is above-average for the venue: abstract, introduction, contribution-isolation, failure analysis, and limitations are all done with care. The honesty about missing controls and the mechanism-level failure-mode taxonomy compensate for the single-seed result. With minor presentation polish (parameter-count derivation in the appendix, tighter table captions, consolidated figure flow around §3.2/§3.3, cross-references from §A.2 to §A.7), this is a clear accept. As-is, weak accept.

## Confidence (1–5)
**4** — I am familiar with the unsupervised panoptic literature (CUPS, U2Seg, S2-UniSeg, CutLER, CutS3D, DepthG, CAUSE) and have evaluated the empirical setup carefully. I did not personally rerun the experiments. Not 5 because I cannot independently verify the parameter count derivation or whether the in-paper monocular-baseline 32.76 PQ is a clean controlled run.

## Questions for Authors

1. **Parameter count derivation.** The abstract and §3.3 say DCFA has ~225K parameters. Could you add the layer-by-layer derivation (input 106, hidden 384, output 90, two hidden layers with LayerNorm) to Appendix B.2 so a reader can verify? With the current spec (`r: R^106 → R^384 → R^384 → R^90`, LayerNorm and ReLU after each hidden layer), I count roughly 41K + 148K + 35K = 223K, which is consistent with 225K — but the paper does not show this. Worth one paragraph in §B.2.

2. **DCFA-only Stage-3 column (§A.4).** The honest disclosure that you don't have a Stage-3 checkpoint trained on DCFA-corrected-but-SIMCF-disabled labels is appreciated. Could you estimate: if you launched this run today, how many GPU-hours and how confident are you that the result would be approximately mid-way between the monocular baseline (32.76) and the full method (35.83)? A back-of-envelope estimate in the rebuttal would help reviewers calibrate the missing control.

3. **+3.07 PQ contribution-isolated framing.** Table 2 reports the Stage-1 advantage grows from +1.31 to +3.07 across Stages 2/3. Is there an interpretable mechanism for this *amplification* — e.g., is the Stage-3 EMA self-training disproportionately effective when seeded with cleaner pseudo-labels? A one-sentence mechanistic claim in §4.2 would strengthen the narrative.

4. **The motorcycle row in §A.6 (PQ=0.1, RQ=0.1, SQ=100).** A near-perfect single instance produces these numbers — please clarify in the table caption that the SQ=100 is the property of one matched instance and RQ=0.1 means 1/N matched. Currently a quick reader may take SQ=100 as a strong claim.

5. **Failure-mode mitigations as future work.** The mitigation column reads as engineering TODOs. Have any been *attempted* during paper development (e.g., the top-K-per-image rule for under-detection)? If so, a one-line note ("we tried the A_min=200 merger filter; it improved over-fragmentation by X PQ but degraded under-detection by Y") would be informative even as a footnote.

6. **Visualization conventions transparency.** §A.7 says void pixels are filled by spatial nearest-neighbor inpainting on the post-Hungarian semantic channel for visualization only. Could you add a small inset in Fig. A.2 or A.3 showing one panel with and without the inpainting, so readers can calibrate how much "visual cleaning" is happening? A small 256×256 inset would do it.

7. **Cross-dataset 19/27-class disclosure on KITTI.** Table 5 footnote ⁂ says "KITTI 19-class and 27-class are identical (KITTI GT only contains the 19 standard classes)." Worth saying this in the main text once, not just in the footnote, because a reviewer may otherwise read both KITTI rows as different evaluations.

8. **Pipeline figure (Fig. 1) — recurring use.** Could the pipeline figure be referenced from §3.2 ("upper branch in Fig. 1"), §3.3 ("DCFA box in Fig. 1"), §3.4 ("SIMCF stage in Fig. 1"), and §3.5 ("Stage 2 box in Fig. 1") so that the figure does the integrative work it deserves? Currently it appears once and then is forgotten.
